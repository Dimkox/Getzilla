## 2026-10-08 — A guard that split on one shell operator missed helpers, abbreviations and expansions

Root cause: the first pass still reasoned about a command through shallow splitting and literal spellings, so git-core helper binaries (`/usr/lib/git-core/git-push`), `git config alias.*` definitions, nested command strings, GNU long-option abbreviations (`rm --rec`, `--no-pres`), brace/variable delete targets (`{~,}`, `"$PWD"`), ANSI-C/quoted secret names (`cat $'\x2eenv'`), recursive readers of secret directories and `gh`/`curl` write verbs all slipped past, while legitimate commit messages and greps mentioning "credentials" were wrongly blocked, and an unbounded glob could time the hook out into fail-open. A policy guard must normalize the whole command into argv (basename, nested commands, abbreviations, expansions) and be proven against adversarial spellings and allow-cases, not one documented example, and must bound its own filesystem work (#36, #37).
